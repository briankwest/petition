Questions

What does signing do?
Signing supports putting the county's resolution to a public vote. It does not commit you to voting yes or no. If enough registered voters sign, the resolution goes on the ballot at the next general county election ({{ cite('62-868', '62 O.S. § 868(H)') }}).
Who may sign?
A registered voter who lives in {{ petition.county }} County. Each person signs only their own name, once. Signing another person's name, signing twice, or signing when not a legal voter is a felony ({{ cite('34-23', '34 O.S. § 23') }}). Check your registration.
How many signatures are needed?
At least ten percent of the registered voters residing in the county ({{ cite('62-868', '62 O.S. § 868(B)(2)') }}). Signed pamphlets must be filed with the County Election Board within 30 days after the resolution is adopted.
Why can't I sign online, or on a printed list at a store?
Oklahoma counts only signatures on official petition pamphlets — a signature sheet attached to a full copy of the petition, witnessed by the circulator, and verified by the circulator's notarized affidavit (34 O.S. §§ {{ cite('34-3', '3') }}, {{ cite('34-6', '6') }}). Signatures on loose or homemade sheets are thrown out ({{ cite('34-6.1', '34 O.S. § 6.1') }}).

Is my signature public?
Yes. A copy of the petition and all signatures are public records under the Oklahoma Open Records Act ({{ cite('34-3', '34 O.S. § 3(B)') }}). This website does not collect or store any voter information.
