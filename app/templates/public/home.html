What the county is being asked to give

The {{ petition.measure.project_name }} was recommended to the Board of County Commissioners on 21 April 2026 and tabled after the June hearings. It would create two incentive districts on about 2,000 acres two miles south of Kiowa and abate most of the new property tax there for a generation. The figures below are the plan's own, with one from the company's annual report.

What binds whom

The plan is carefully drafted. The problem is narrower: the terms that protect the county are labelled illustrative, and the terms that bind the county are already definite.

  • Definite, on the county. The {{ petition.fmt.abatement }}, the 25 years, the 1 January 2026 base value, and five taxing-entity signatures within 60 days of adoption.
  • Illustrative, on the company. The minimum payments, which the plan introduces as "a non-binding example" that is "not intended to establish fixed or binding payment obligations."
  • Absent. A jobs floor, a capacity minimum, a parent guarantee, a recapture clause if the company leaves, and any word on water source, backup generators or noise.

What a referendum does

Under the Oklahoma Local Development Act, voters can refer a county resolution like this one to the ballot. If the Board adopts the plan, a petition signed by ten percent of the county's registered voters{% if targets.legal_minimum %}, {{ targets.legal_minimum|int }} of {{ targets.registered_voters|int }},{% endif %} must be filed within 30 days.{% if targets.target %} We are aiming for {{ targets.target|int }} signatures{% if targets.overcollect_pct %}, {{ targets.overcollect_pct }} percent over the minimum,{% endif %} so that rejected signatures cannot sink the filing.{% endif %} The question then goes to the next general county election, and the abatement waits for the answer (62 O.S. § 868).

The Board has tabled the resolution. Nothing can be signed until it acts, and nothing is being circulated. This site is ready for the day it does.
