01 / Brief

The case in ten lines

every line carries its source
  1. Pittsburg County is asked to abate up to 85 percent of new value for twenty-five years. Childress County, Texas got IREN’s 750 MW campus on ten years, with a written “shall not extend beyond ten.”C1C5C6
  2. The minimum payments that would protect the county are introduced as a non-binding example, not intended to establish fixed or binding payment obligations. The 85 percent, the 25 years and the base value date are definite.C1
  3. Base value is the assessed value as of 1 January 2026, months before any vote, and stays frozen for the full term.C1
  4. The company may terminate the Tax Incentive Agreement on 90 days’ notice. No recapture clause is stated.C1
  5. The plan anticipates 40 jobs per phase and binds the company to none. Each Childress agreement requires “no less than ten.”C1C5
  6. The counterparty is Emerald ProjectCo Inc., a Delaware corporation formed 29 July 2025 and capitalised at ten dollars, whose Oklahoma qualification is signed by Will Roberts as President from IREN’s Childress campus. No owner is on any record and the plan names no guarantor. Childress bound five IREN companies jointly and severally.C24C25C5
  7. The plan is built on 1,200 MW. IREN tells its shareholders the Oklahoma campus is 1,600 MW on 2,000 acres. The top abatement tier starts above 550 MW per phase and carries the same illustrative minimum, so the extra 400 MW pays the county nothing more.C1C11C13
  8. Exhibits C and D, existing conditions and proposed improvements, are blank in the copy the county published.C1
  9. The plan never mentions 68 O.S. § 2902, under which the state reimburses local taxing entities for exempt manufacturing property, and the Tax Commission’s 2026 report lists NAICS 5182 data-center facilities among the qualifying categories.C1C22C23
  10. IREN closed FY26 with $5.90 billion of unrestricted cash, raised $11.0 billion in twelve months at a blended coupon under one percent, and tells shareholders recent contracts pay back in about two years. The county’s committee found the investment would “more likely than not not occur” without the districts.C11C12C2

04 / Questions

The fifteen questions

grounds under each

Each question is phrased so the answer is either a document or an admission that there is none. The grounds line names where the fact behind it sits.

The counterparty

  1. Who owns Emerald ProjectCo Inc., and will IREN Limited guarantee its obligations?

    The certified Oklahoma qualification is signed by Will Roberts as President, the name of IREN’s co-founder and co-chief executive, from IREN’s Childress campus address; it declares $21,100 of assets and ten dollars of authorised capital, and names no shareholder. IREN’s own list of significant subsidiaries does not include the company. In Childress County five IREN companies signed, each a party and all jointly and severally liable. The plan names one company and no guarantor.

    GroundsProject Plan · Exhibit 21.1 to IREN’s 10-K · Delaware file 10276559 · Oklahoma filing 2313901183 · Childress Phase 1 agreementC1I2C24C25C5

  2. Who has the county been dealing with, and does the officer who signed in Oklahoma stand behind the plan?

    The Oklahoma qualification is signed by Will Roberts as President and Jason Date as Secretary, and the registered-agent change by William Roberts; Jason Date does not appear in IREN’s filings. The plan was prepared with the county’s counsel and recommended by the committee. The record should say who represented the applicant at each step, and whether the president who signed in Oklahoma, or IREN Limited itself, will sign the Tax Incentive Agreement.

    GroundsProject Plan · committee recommendation of 21 April 2026 · Oklahoma filing 2313901183C1C2C25

What binds whom

  1. Will the Board adopt anything before the minimum payments are made binding, and will the Tax Incentive Agreement be published before the vote?

    The plan introduces its minimum-payment table as “a non-binding example,” “intended solely to demonstrate the general economic framework,” and “not intended to establish fixed or binding payment obligations.” The Economic Development Agreement and the Tax Incentive Agreement do not exist in public form.

    GroundsProject Plan, minimum payment table and § 12C1

  2. If the company terminates on ninety days’ notice in year three, what does the county recover?

    The plan lets the company terminate the Tax Incentive Agreement on ninety days’ notice and does not say what is recaptured. Childress County wrote cancellation and recapture, less payments made, into its agreements as the county’s remedy.

    GroundsProject Plan · Childress Phase 1 and Phase 2 agreementsC1C5C6

  3. Why twenty-five years?

    Childress County abated for ten calendar years and wrote “shall not extend beyond ten” into the agreement. IREN built 750 MW there against 600 committed, on its own land, with its own substations.

    GroundsProject Plan § 8.5 · Childress agreements · IREN’s Childress site pageC1C5C6C13

  4. Will the base value be reset to the adoption date?

    Base Value is the assessed value as of 1 January 2026, certified within ninety days of adoption and then frozen for twenty-five years. Every dollar of value added to the site since January is abated at up to 85 percent.

    GroundsProject Plan, definitions and § 8C1

The numbers

  1. If the company builds 1,600 MW rather than 1,200, what does the county receive for the extra 400?

    The plan’s cost tables, tiers and payment schedule assume two phases of about 600 MW. IREN’s annual report and site page describe the Oklahoma campus as 1,600 MW across 2,000 acres. The top tier begins above 550 MW per phase and carries the same illustrative minimum.

    GroundsProject Plan § 8.6 and § 10.1 · IREN FY26 10-K · IREN’s Oklahoma site pageC1C11C13

  2. Will Exhibits C and D be completed and republished before adoption?

    Exhibit C, “Existing Uses and Conditions,” and Exhibit D, “Proposed Improvements and Uses,” are empty but for their headings in the copy the county published. The document meant to record what is on the land now, and what will be on it afterwards, says neither.

    GroundsProject Plan, Exhibits C and DC1

  3. Has the county asked whether this project qualifies under 68 O.S. § 2902, where the state reimburses the county, and what the county’s loss is under that route compared with this one?

    The plan does not mention § 2902. The Tax Commission’s 2026 report lists NAICS 5182 data-center facilities among the categories reimbursed from the Ad Valorem Reimbursement Fund, subject to an out-of-state revenue test, and notes that a NAICS code alone does not establish eligibility. The comparison is not on the record.

    GroundsProject Plan · 68 O.S. § 2902 · Oklahoma Tax Commission, 2026 Ad Valorem Annual ReportC1C22C23

  4. Will there be a written jobs floor with a clawback?

    The plan “anticipates” 40 ongoing jobs per phase and binds the company to none. Each Childress agreement requires “no less than ten (10) new, full-time equivalent jobs.” Both counties’ documents already say permanent employment will be small.

    GroundsProject Plan · Childress Phase 1, 2 and 3 agreementsC1C5C6C7

  5. Who pays for transmission and substation upgrades, and how much water is drawn, from where?

    Neither county document states who bears the grid costs or what the water demand is. In Childress the company put in its own substations. The Oklahoma plan is silent on both.

    GroundsProject Plan · IREN’s Childress site pageC1C13

Process and need

  1. Have the five taxing entities been shown the plan’s own statement that the payments are illustrative?

    Kiowa Public Schools, the county, the Health Department, Kiamichi Technology Center and the Southern Oklahoma Public Library System must sign the Tax Incentive Agreement within sixty days of adoption, and no district activates until all have. The Phase 1 distribution the plan shows them, $11.0 million to Kiowa Public Schools among it, is labelled non-binding.

    GroundsProject Plan, Tax Incentive Agreement clause and distribution tableC1

  2. What evidence supported the finding that the investment “would more likely than not not occur” without the districts?

    IREN closed FY26 with $5.90 billion of unrestricted cash, raised $11.0 billion in twelve months at a blended coupon under one percent, tells shareholders recent contracts pay back in about two years, and built Childress on a ten-year deal. The committee’s finding is the statutory basis for the districts.

    GroundsCommittee recommendation, findings under 62 O.S. § 855 · IREN FY26 10-K and results release · Childress Phase 1 agreementC2C11C12C5

  3. When does the tabled resolution return to the agenda, and how many days before will the two agreements be published?

    The notice of 18 May 2026 set hearings for 8 and 22 June. No adopted resolution appears on the county’s resolutions page, and no revised plan has been published. The public should not learn the terms from an agenda packet.

    GroundsNotice of public hearings · Project PlanC3C1

Water, power and noise

  1. What water will the campus draw, from whom, what is in the loop, and where does it go when drained?

    The plan is silent on water. IREN told the McAlester News-Capital the loop is filled once, tops up at less than five households a year, disperses heat through air, uses no PFAS coolant, and would truck water in if local supply fell short, as at Childress, where its chief operating officer says the campus was filled with water “from outside the county.” None of that is in any document the county holds, no third party has measured the Childress draw, and under SB 259 a data center that buys water from a utility needs no permit and reports nothing. Will the Tax Incentive Agreement name the source and supplier, the initial fill and the annual top-up, the fluid and its disposal route, the number and permits of backup generators, and a noise limit at the property line?

    GroundsProject Plan · IREN Q&A, McAlester News-Capital · IREN’s chief operating officer on Childress · SB 259 and the water board’s director · the Greenbelt recordC1C34C30C37C36C27

05 / Conditions

What would answer them

None of this asks the Board to refuse growth. It asks the Board to put the county’s protections in the same class of document as the county’s obligations. Each condition below is something Childress County, Texas already has in writing, or something the plan itself makes necessary.

  • 01Strike “non-binding example” and write the minimum-payment table into the Tax Incentive Agreement as a floor.
  • 02IREN Limited as guarantor, and every affiliate holding land or equipment in the districts jointly and severally liable, as in Childress.
  • 03Base value set at the adoption date, not 1 January 2026.
  • 04Term capped at ten years, with “shall not extend beyond ten.”
  • 05Recapture on early termination, and a stay-in-place covenant for improvements, as in Childress.
  • 06A written jobs floor with clawback, and a capacity minimum per phase.
  • 07Exhibits C and D completed, and both agreements published at least thirty days before any vote.
  • 08A § 2902 comparison on the record before the taxing entities are asked to sign.
  • 09Water, power and generators in the agreement: source and supplier, initial fill and annual top-up, the fluid and its disposal route, generator count and permits, and a noise limit at the property line.
