Childress vs. Kiowa What IREN signed in Texas · what Pittsburg County is being asked for · and what the company’s own balance sheet says about the need

IREN has been building at Childress, Texas since 2022. It owns the land freehold, it owns the substations, it runs 750 megawatts, and it did all of that on a deal worth about a million dollars a year to Childress County. The same company closed its last financial year holding $5.90 billion in unrestricted cash, having raised $11.0 billion in twelve months. This file compares the two deals, side by side, from the documents — and the comparison keeps returning to one question: what is the abatement buying that the company would not do anyway?

01 / Capacity

What the company can already afford

FY26 · year ended 30 Jun 2026

Before comparing two tax deals it is worth settling the question they are both supposed to answer. An abatement is justified by necessity. So take the necessity first, from the company’s own audited accounts.

At 30 June 2026 IREN held $5,895.6m of unrestricted cash and a further $1,723.9m restricted inside its GPU financing subsidiary. During that year it raised $4,742.8m from issuing shares and $6,299.6m from convertible notes — $11.0 billion in twelve months — and the notes carry roughly $59.5m of annual coupon on $6.75bn of principal, a blended rate of about 0.88%.11

More to the point, the company tells its own shareholders what this business earns. In the FY26 results release IREN said recent three-year contracts price at above $20 million of revenue per megawatt of IT load, implying roughly a two-year payback, with active discussions at about $25 million per MW; 2026 capacity is described as largely sold out.12

The arithmetic that frames everything below

A project that returns its capital in about two years is being offered a tax holiday lasting twenty-five. Whatever else the Pittsburg County proposal is, it is not the difference between the data center being built and not being built — and the best evidence for that is four hundred miles south-west, in Childress County, where the same company built a bigger campus on a shorter, smaller deal.

The full company record — the board, the voting control, the pay, the $9.7bn Microsoft contract traced to the signed document — is in The IREN File.

02 / Texas

Childress: what IREN signed, and then built

Operating since 2022

Childress County is the control group, and an unusually good one: the agreements are executed, they are public, and anyone can download them from the county’s own website.4

IREN — then trading as Iris Energy — entered three tax abatement agreements with Childress County under Chapter 312 of the Texas Tax Code. The counterparty is not a single project company. It is IE US Development Holdings 3 Inc. together with IE US Hardware 1, 2, 3 and 4 Inc., all Delaware corporations, and the agreements state that each is a party and all are jointly and severally liable. Notices go to Will Roberts at IREN’s Sydney head office.56

Each agreement abates 100% of county property taxes on eligible property for ten calendar years and says so twice, adding that the abatement “shall not extend beyond ten (10) Calendar Years.” Pre-existing value stays fully taxable throughout, and the property returns to the roll in full at the end. Improvements other than servers and transformers must remain in place for twenty years after commercial operation. If the company misses its deadlines the county may cancel and recapture the abated taxes less the payments already made — and that recapture is expressly the county’s sole remedy.67

Childress County describes the combined project in its own statutory notice as approximately $1.4 billion of improvements within Iris Energy Reinvestment Zone #2, which it created in October 2024 after IREN bought additional land adjoining the original zone.9

Two details are worth holding onto. The county was not the only party at the table: Clarendon College negotiated its own agreements, and its Phase 3 payment runs at $145 per megawatt per year — about $29,000 a year at 200 MW. Every taxing entity got its own signed instrument.8 And the deadline to reach commercial operation was a hard calendar date in each agreement, extendable by exactly one year on written notice and a $25,000 fee.67

Then the company built more than it promised

The three agreements commit to 600 MW and about $1.395 billion. IREN’s own site page now describes Childress as 750 MW on a 576-acre freehold site, with dual diverse fiber and on-site substations owned by IREN.13 It is the beachhead for the Microsoft work — four liquid-cooled “Horizon” halls, the first of which was delivered and accepted in August 2026.11 The company bought the land outright, built the substations itself, and exceeded the contracted capacity by a quarter. It did that on ten years, not twenty-five.

03 / Oklahoma

Kiowa: what Pittsburg County is being asked for

Recommended 21 Apr 2026 · not adopted

The Pittsburg County proposal is not a Texas-style abatement scaled up. It is a different instrument, running four times longer, over a project the plan values at a different order of magnitude.

The Emerald ProjectCo Data Center Project Economic Development Project Plan was prepared for the Board of County Commissioners with the assistance of Floyd & Driver, PLLC of Norman, and recommended for approval by the county’s Statutory Review Committee on 21 April 2026 under the Oklahoma Local Development Act.12 It creates two Incentive Districts over approximately 2,000 acres about two miles south of Kiowa along US 69, one district per phase.13

The abatement is a sliding scale, recalculated every year against megawatts actually energized, applied to value above a fixed Base Value:

The Term is twenty-five years from each district’s Activation Date, and the districts may be activated independently and in any order. A district cannot activate before 1 January 2028, and requires at least 50 MW energized — or reasonably expected within twelve months — together with capital investment in that district exceeding one billion dollars. All districts must be activated within ten years of the adopting resolution.1

The Base Value is the assessed value of the project site as of 1 January 2026, certified by the County Assessor within ninety days of adoption and then fixed for the entire twenty-five years. Everything above it is what gets abated.1

The scale is the part that does not resemble Childress at all. The plan’s own cost table puts each phase at approximately twenty-five billion dollars:

Two phases at that figure is fifty billion dollars against Childress’s $1.395 billion — thirty-six times the capital, on three and a half times the land, for two and a half times as long.15

One clause deserves separate notice. Within sixty days of adoption the company must enter a Tax Incentive Agreement with the county and every affected Taxing Entity — Kiowa Public Schools, Pittsburg County, the Pittsburg County Health Department, Kiamichi Technology Center and the Southern Oklahoma Public Library System — and no district activates until all of them have signed. But the plan also provides that the company may terminate that agreement on not less than ninety days’ written notice, at which point the incentives end and accrued payments survive.1

06 / Jobs

Both documents already answer the jobs question

in their own words

This is the part nobody has to argue about. The number is written down twice, by two different sets of lawyers, in two states, and neither of them is hiding it.

Every one of the three Childress agreements carries the same representation. If the company builds the improvements and reaches commercial operation, the project will add at least $500,000 to the tax roll and will:

“create no less than ten (10) new, full-time equivalent jobs”
Childress County — Iris Energy, Amended and Restated Tax Abatement Agreement, Phase 2, § V(C). The identical sentence appears in Phase 1 and Phase 3.567

Ten jobs for a 300-megawatt, $592.5 million phase. Thirty across the whole 600 MW campus, if none of them overlap. That is the enforceable employment consequence of Childress County giving up all of its property tax on the site for a decade.567

Pittsburg County’s Project Plan is blunter still. Section 12.5 is headed “Financial Impact on Business Entities,” and it explains why the county should not worry about the cost of new residents:

“Primarily due to the relatively low number of permanent employees anticipated, overall long term employment opportunities created will be minimal, which mitigates the impact on the affected Taxing Entities…”
Emerald ProjectCo Data Center Project Economic Development Project Plan, § 12.5, page 22 of 271

The next paragraph gives the number:

“The Project is anticipated to create 40 ongoing jobs per Phase, along with thousands of construction jobs…”
Same plan, § 12.5, page 23 of 271

Forty against thousands. The county’s own consultants wrote the cliff into the plan and then listed the small permanent workforce as a point in the project’s favour — because few permanent workers means few new children in Kiowa Public Schools and few new calls on the Health Department. As a fiscal argument that is entirely coherent. As an answer to “what does this do for people who live here,” it is the whole answer, and it is already on the record.1

07 / Aftermath

What eight years of it did to Childress County

measured, not predicted

Childress is the only place where the question can be answered with evidence instead of argument. IREN has been building and operating there since 2022. So: what happened to the county?

First, the thing this file will not claim

The obvious worry about a data center in a small county is that it prices people out of their homes. In Childress, the census record does not support that, and it should not be argued.

Rents and house prices in Childress rose more slowly than in the typical Texas county over exactly the years the data center was built and energized. And in a county of 6,700 the survey’s own margins of error are wide enough that none of those changes is statistically significant at the 90% level — the rent estimate for 2024 is $913 give or take $209.17

Anyone who argues that IREN drove up rents in Childress can be refuted in an afternoon with public data, and the rest of the case would go down with it. The real findings are below, and they are stronger.

The population went up, then went back down

Childress County gained people while the site was being built and lost them once the first phase was done. The Census Bureau’s annual estimates put net migration at +164 in 2021 and +78 in 2022 — then −62 in 2023 and −59 in 2024. County population peaked at 6,834 in 2022 and was back to 6,679 by 2024, below where it started the decade.18

This is the jobs cliff, drawn from the census rather than from an argument. The crowd arrives, and then it leaves.

Nobody built any housing

Across the entire period — 2018 through 2025 — the Census Bureau’s Building Permits Survey records zero new housing units authorized in Childress County. Not one, in any year, of any type.19

That is not a gap in the survey. In the 2024 file, 188 of the 223 Texas counties listed reported at least one authorized unit, and Childress’s small neighbours are among them: Donley authorized three, Dallam five, Hardeman one.19 A county that gains a $1.4 billion capital project9 and builds no housing is a county whose workforce did not move in.

Where the jobs actually landed

The Bureau of Labor Statistics counts every job covered by unemployment insurance, by industry, by county. It is the closest thing to a headcount that exists, and for Childress it says something the brochures do not.

Childress County added $901.5 million of market value between 2021 and 2025. Of that, $221.2 million reached the taxable roll. Roughly three quarters of the new value never became taxable, and the gap between what the county is worth and what it can tax widened from $588 million to $1.27 billion.21

The honest qualification, stated because it matters: that gap is not all abatement. It includes homestead and over-65 exemptions, disabled-veteran exemptions, and the agricultural and open-space valuations that dominate a ranching county. What can be said is narrower and still striking — the gap grew by $680 million in four years, in a county that was losing population, and agricultural valuations do not move like that.

And the counterweight, which belongs here for the same reason: Childress County came out ahead in cash. Its tax rate fell from 0.578882 to 0.535000 and its levy still rose, to $3.96 million in 2025 from $3.43 million the year before.21 A county can abate most of a project and still collect more than it did — which is exactly the argument that will be made in Pittsburg County, and it is not a foolish one.

The schools, which is the part that does not transfer

Then there is the finding that changes how the Kiowa proposal should be read.

Childress ISD’s taxable value more than doubled while the county’s abatement gap was widening.21 The school district was not inside the county’s deal. The county’s own agreements make that arrangement explicit: Phase 1 carries a separate $50,000 annual Education Program Payment which the County undertakes to distribute to the independent school districts and community colleges serving county residents — a payment made to the schools precisely because their levy was not being abated. Clarendon College, likewise, negotiated its own agreement rather than being folded into the county’s.58

In Childress the biggest levy stayed whole. In the Kiowa plan it does not

Pittsburg County’s Project Plan puts Kiowa Public Schools first among the Taxing Entities, requires the district to execute the Tax Incentive Agreement before any district can activate, and assigns it 55% of the illustrative distribution — $11.0 million of a $20 million example.1 The school district is not being paid alongside the abatement, as in Childress. It is inside it, for twenty-five years.

That is the single largest structural difference between the deal IREN signed in Texas and the one being proposed here, and it is worth putting to the Board and to the school board in those terms.

What none of this isolates

Childress County hosts more than IREN. Lancium holds county abatement agreements covering ten permitted phases, and Greenalia operates abated solar and wind there.4 Every figure in this section is a county total. None of it separates IREN’s effect from the others’, and nothing here should be quoted as though it did. What the section establishes is what happened to the county that hosts them — which is the question Pittsburg County is actually facing.

The headline percentages mislead, and this is the point of putting them side by side. Childress abated one hundred percent and Kiowa proposes eighty-five — yet after payments in lieu the two counties retain almost the same share, 13% against 18%. What actually differs is duration, scale and who is inside the deal: ten years against twenty-five, $1.4 billion of investment against $50 billion, and a school district left whole against a school district made a signatory.

What this is based on

Every figure above is derived from a published document. The derivations are short enough to check by hand, and they are set out here so that they can be.

Childress

  • Investment: $1,395,000,000. The sum of the capital investment each executed agreement states for its phase — $197.5m for Phase 1, $592.5m for Phase 2, $605.0m for Phase 3.567
  • Rate: 0.535000 per $100. Childress County’s total tax rate for 2025 in the Texas Comptroller’s County Report of Property Value. Texas assesses at full market value, so the county tax on fully taxable improvements is investment × 0.535 ÷ 100 = $7,463,250 a year.21
  • The rate convention is verified, not assumed. Applying it to the county’s own 2025 taxable value of $740,245,541 gives $3,960,314 — which is the calculated levy the Comptroller publishes for Childress County, to the dollar.21
  • Payments: $1,000,000 a year. The three PILOT formulas applied to the capacity each agreement commits to: $1,250 × 100 MW; $75,000 + ($1,250 × 300 MW); $50,000 + ($1,625 × 200 MW). Plus the $50,000 Education Program Payment in Phase 1.567

Kiowa

  • Received: $367,200,000 per phase. The Project Plan’s own § 12.4 estimate of additional ad valorem over the life of the Incentive Districts, per phase: $204m to Kiowa Public Schools, $70m to the county, $60.6m to Kiamichi Technology Center, $20m to the library system, $12.6m to the Health Department.1
  • Grossing up: ÷ 0.15. At more than 550 MW energized the abatement percentage is 85% under § 8.6, so what the entities receive is the remaining 15%. Dividing gives a but-for liability of $2,448,000,000 per phase, and $4.896bn for the two phases the plan contemplates.1
  • The gross-up is cross-checked against the plan’s own floor. It implies $34.4 million a year to public bodies across both phases. The plan’s illustrative stabilized minimum is $15 million per phase, plus a $5 million Community Betterment Payment — $35 million a year. Two independent routes through the same document agree within 3%.1
  • Community Betterment Payment: $5,000,000 a year. The § 12.2 tier for more than 550 MW. The plan is explicit that only one CBP is owed for the whole project however many districts activate, so it is counted once, not twice. It is also CPI-adjusted from 2032, which this calculation does not attempt to model — so the CBP line is, if anything, understated.1

Four things this calculation does not claim

  • “Without” prices the abatement; it is not a forecast of revenue. The Review Committee found it more likely than not that the investment would not occur without the districts.2 This file questions that finding rather than assuming it away. What the table measures is what the relief is worth — which is a different and answerable question.
  • The Project Plan’s own numbers are labelled illustrative. § 12.4’s estimates and § 12.1’s distribution are examples, and the plan says the operative provisions control. Building on them is the best available method; it is not the same as building on a contract.1
  • There is an ambiguity in the plan that no outside reader can resolve. “Required Payment” is defined as the greater of the minimum or the amount determined by applying the Abatement Percentage to the But-For Tax Liability. Read literally that is 85% of the but-for liability, not the 15% the abatement scale implies. This calculation uses 15%, consistent with § 8.6 — and the ambiguity belongs on the question list.1
  • Neither column models depreciation, phasing or the introductory period. Childress’s figure assumes the committed capacity is built and appraised at the stated investment; Kiowa’s assumes the 85% tier throughout. Real schedules ramp and real equipment depreciates, which moves both columns in the same direction and does not change their ratio.

And the alternative nobody has costed: the state already pays for this

Oklahoma has a five-year ad valorem exemption for qualifying facilities under 68 O.S. § 2902, and under 62 O.S. § 193 the state — not the county — reimburses the local taxing jurisdictions for the revenue they lose to it, out of the Ad Valorem Reimbursement Fund. Pittsburg County claims from that fund today: $22,244 for one manufacturer in the 2026 report.2223

The Tax Commission’s current list of qualifying categories includes establishments “as further defined under Industry Number 5182 of the latest NAICS Manual, which derive at least eighty percent (80%) of gross annual revenues from the sale of a product or service to an out-of-state buyer or consumer.” NAICS 5182 is data processing and hosting. IREN’s named counterparties are Microsoft, NVIDIA, Prometheus, Perplexity and Together AI.2311

The Emerald ProjectCo Project Plan does not mention § 2902 anywhere.1 Two cautions belong with that, and they are the company’s and the county’s to answer rather than this file’s: the Tax Commission states plainly that a NAICS classification alone does not establish eligibility, and § 2902’s specific data-centre paragraph carries a placed-in-service deadline of 31 December 2021, so which route would apply is a question for counsel.2223

The question for the Board is nevertheless simple enough to put in one sentence: before granting twenty-five years of local relief that nobody reimburses, was five years of state relief that the state reimburses ever examined, and if so, where is that analysis?

09 / Adrift

Four things in the file that do not add up

Four hundred megawatts are missing

The Project Plan is built throughout on two phases of approximately 600 MW each — 1,200 MW. Its cost tables, its abatement tiers and its payment schedule all assume it.1 IREN’s own site page and its annual report describe the Oklahoma campus as 1,600 MW across 2,000 acres.1311 Nothing published reconciles the two — and because the abatement tiers stop at “greater than 550 MW,” a project forty percent larger pays on the same top tier.

Two exhibits are blank

Exhibit C of the Project Plan is titled “Existing Uses and Conditions.” Exhibit D is titled “Proposed Improvements and Uses.” In the copy the county published, both pages are empty but for their headings. The document meant to record what is on the land now, and what will be on it afterwards, says neither.1

The base value is already fixed

Base Value is the assessed value of the project site as of 1 January 2026, certified within ninety days of adoption and then frozen for the whole twenty-five-year term. Everything above it is what gets abated. The land was being assembled through 2025 and 2026; the clock on the county’s share was set before any vote was taken.1

Childress got joint and several liability; the Oklahoma plan names one company and no guarantor

The Texas agreements bind five Delaware corporations and state that each is a party and all are jointly and severally liable.56 The Pittsburg County plan names Emerald ProjectCo Inc. alone. That name does not appear among the seven significant subsidiaries IREN lists in its annual report, and the plan contains no parent guarantee.111 Those seven are all on the registers: five Delaware entities filed between July 2021 and February 2026, every one with Corporation Service Company as agent, and two British Columbia companies registered in 2021 and 2023.2426

The state registries supply what the plan does not, and it is not much. Emerald ProjectCo Inc. was incorporated in Delaware on 29 July 2025 and qualified to do business in Oklahoma as a foreign for-profit business corporation on 16 December 2025, four months before the Statutory Review Committee’s recommendation. Neither record names an officer, a director, a shareholder or a parent.2425

10 / Reading

The fair reading, which cuts both ways

A comparison is only worth publishing if it can surprise the person who commissioned it, so here is the part that argues against the simple version of this story.

The Oklahoma structure is better drafted than the Texas one. It is not a naive giveaway. It ties the abatement to megawatts actually energized and recalculates it every year. It sets a floor under the county’s revenue instead of relying on assessed value alone. It counts business personal property — the servers, the racks, the networking gear, the most valuable and most mobile thing on the site — regardless of ownership or leasing structure, and forbids the company from structuring around it. It gives the county audit rights and drops the company to the lowest abatement tier if it fails to certify its megawatts. Childress County got none of that. Childress County got a hundred percent off for ten years and $1,667 per megawatt.16

So the case against this deal is not that Pittsburg County’s lawyers were careless. It is narrower and harder to answer: the parts that protect the county are the parts labelled illustrative, and the parts that bind the county are the parts that are already definite. Eighty-five percent is definite. Twenty-five years is definite. The 1 January 2026 base value is definite. The right to terminate on ninety days’ notice is definite. The fifteen million dollars is an example.1

Which is why the sequence matters. Those five taxing-entity signatures — the schools, the county, the health department, the technology center, the library system — are the county’s entire leverage, and they are spent the moment the plan is adopted. Childress County put its numbers in the contract first. Pittsburg County is being asked to adopt the framework and negotiate the numbers afterwards.16

And underneath all of it, the question that does not go away

Childress, Texas is the answer to whether this company builds without a twenty-five-year tax holiday. It built there on ten years. It bought the land freehold, put in its own substations, and ended up running 750 megawatts against 600 committed — a bigger campus than it promised, on a shorter and smaller deal than the one now on the table here.136

The company that did that closed its last financial year with $5.90 billion of unrestricted cash, having raised $11.0 billion in twelve months at a blended coupon under one percent, and it tells its own shareholders that recent contracts pay back the capital in about two years.1112 Pittsburg County is not the reason this project happens or does not happen. It is being asked to contribute to something that is already funded — and the honest question for the Board is not whether the drafting is good, but what the abatement buys that the company would not do anyway.
