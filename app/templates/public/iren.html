01 / Business

What the company actually does

FY26 · year ended 30 Jun 2026

IREN describes itself as a vertically integrated AI Cloud Services platform that owns all three layers of the stack: the data center layer (land, power, substations, buildings, cooling), the compute layer (GPUs, CPUs, storage, servers, networking), and the software layer (managed services and enterprise support, acquired wholesale with Mirantis in August 2026).1

The distinguishing structural choice is grid-connected power rather than behind-the-meter generation. IREN buys land and secures transmission interconnection directly — regulated tariffs from BC Hydro in British Columbia and Public Service Company of Oklahoma in Oklahoma, wholesale market exposure through ERCOT in Texas — and builds the substations itself. Every operating site has run on 100% renewable energy since commencement, roughly 98% from clean generation in British Columbia with the balance covered by renewable energy certificates.1

The company is simultaneously exiting the business that built it. As of 30 June 2026 installed bitcoin mining capacity was 23.2 EH/s occupying about 380 MW, against just 40 MW of operating AI cloud capacity. IREN began decommissioning miners during FY26 and aims to substantially complete the transition by 31 December 2026. It liquidates mined bitcoin daily and held none on its balance sheet at year end.1

Site notes

  • Childress is the AI beachhead. It hosts the four "Horizon" liquid-cooled halls built for Microsoft, each 50 MW of critical IT load. Horizon 1 was delivered and accepted in August 2026; Horizon 2 is commissioning and Horizons 3–4 are in late-stage construction, targeting Q4 2026.

  • New liquid-cooled deployments are planned at Mackenzie, Canal Flats and Prince George during 2027 to use power headroom already secured at the operating British Columbia sites.

People, and where they sit

Headcount was 685 employees at 30 June 2026 — nearly tripled during FY26, including more than 60 who arrived with Nostrum. The Mirantis acquisition added roughly 580 more in August 2026, taking the group past 1,250. Separately, about 3,000 personnel including contractors and subcontractors were engaged across the US sites at year end. No employees are unionised.

The asset base tells the geographic story more honestly than the head office does: 72% of non-current assets sit in the United States and 24% in Canada, while the registered office, the executive filing address and the fiscal calendar are all Australian.1

Officer locations: what the record actually supports

Every Section 16 insider — both Roberts brothers, Bartholomew, Alfred, Guzowski, Parasuraman, Lewis and Nucifora — files Forms 3, 4 and 144 from the same c/o address at 55 Market Street, Sydney. No individual residential address appears in any IREN filing, and none is required to.13

Individual locations are stated publicly for only two people: David Bartholomew is described as Australia-based, and Chris Parker was announced as Sydney-based on appointment.520 Anything further would be inference rather than record.

05 / Control

Two unregistered shares hold 43.5% of the vote

The most consequential governance fact about IREN is not in the ownership percentages. It is in the two B Class shares, held by the Roberts brothers' family trusts.

Under the constitution, each holder of a B Class share is entitled to 15 votes per Ordinary share held by that holder. B Class shares carry no dividend rights and are unregistered. They must be redeemed on the earliest of: the holder ceasing to be a director through voluntary retirement, a transfer in breach of the constitution, liquidation, or 17 November 2033 — twelve years after listing.

The practical effect: Daniel and Will Roberts each hold 2.3% of ordinary shares but 21.8% of total voting power. Directors and executive officers together held 4.6% of the ordinary shares and 43.5% of the vote. Constitutional amendments require 75% of votes cast.35

This is direct parent recourse, and it is the liability sitting opposite the $9.7bn of Microsoft revenue. The two documents should always be read together.8

NVIDIA is simultaneously IREN's chip supplier, a cloud customer, a certifying body and a prospective shareholder. Under the May 2026 strategic partnership, NVIDIA holds the right to invest up to $2.1 billion in ordinary shares, contingent on delivery of up to 600,000 GPUs and regulatory conditions, supporting up to 5 GW of DSX-aligned infrastructure. IREN is an NVIDIA Cloud Partner, secured Preferred Partner status in FY26, and holds Exemplar Cloud status for HGX B300 and GB300 NVL72 — the latter awarded after NVIDIA tested the Horizon 1 deployment at Childress in August 2026.1

  • $2.8bn of new GPU financings for non-investment-grade customer deployments, including $2.4bn led by Blue Owl and PIMCO at a 9.0% fixed rate for the Mackenzie air-cooled expansion — covering 90% of that GPU capex.

The contract's own arithmetic closes

What is redacted, and what isn't

83 passages are marked [***] under the standard that the information "is not material and/or contains personal information." The redactions cover per-tranche values, GPU quantities, delivery dates, the price per GPU-hour, and site addresses. The aggregate economics — the numbers above — are not redacted. The hardest corroboration: Microsoft's cash is already on the balance sheet

Note 20 discloses $1,623,480 thousand of deferred lease revenue — advance payments received on capacity tranches that had not commenced as of 30 June 2026. Against a contractual aggregate upfront of $1,932,839,884.80, that is 84% of the prepayment already received in cash, before a single tranche started earning.

One honest caveat: that line item may include prepayments from other lease-classified customers, so 84% is an upper bound on the share attributable to Microsoft. The direction is not in doubt — over $1.6bn of customer money has actually been wired.17

And the audited aggregate reconciles

The FY26 accounts disclose $5.1bn of unsatisfied remaining performance obligations under ASC 606 plus $11.4bn of aggregate contracted lease value under ASC 842 — $16.6bn in total. Announced contracts sum to roughly $15.9bn (Microsoft $9.7bn, NVIDIA $3.4bn, the July trio $2.8bn) with several undisclosed deals on top. The public numbers and the audited number agree.148

Why Microsoft sits outside the RPO figure

An accounting point worth understanding before comparing IREN to peers. IREN determined that its GPU capacity contracts contain leases — of specified GPU equipment and dedicated data center space — and accounts for them as operating leases with itself as lessor under ASC 842. That places the Microsoft value in the $11.4bn lease bucket rather than in the $5.1bn ASC 606 RPO. Anyone reading RPO alone as "the backlog" will understate it by roughly two-thirds.1

The caveat that matters most: none of it is revenue yet

The accounts state plainly that "No lease revenue was recognized during the periods presented", and that all FY26 AI Cloud Services revenue was generated in Canada. Microsoft contributed zero to the $128.8m of FY26 AI Cloud revenue — that figure comes entirely from the older British Columbia deployments.

Revenue begins only on customer acceptance. Horizon 1 was accepted in August 2026, which falls in FY27. Of the ASC 606 RPO, only $0.9bn is expected to be recognized in the twelve months to 30 June 2027 and $1.3bn in the year after.1

Verdict: the $9.7bn is real as an executed contract and substantially real as cash in the bank. It is not yet real as revenue, and the gap between those two states is where the execution risk lives.

12 / Exposure

Litigation, tax and audit history

NYDIG receivership — settled

In February 2023 PricewaterhouseCoopers was appointed receiver to two IREN non-recourse SPVs at the instance of lender NYDIG, spawning parallel proceedings in the Supreme Court of British Columbia, the BC Court of Appeal and the Federal Court of Australia. The BC Supreme Court initially declared certain hashpower transactions fraudulent conveyances; the Court of Appeal set that declaration aside in June 2024 while upholding dismissal of NYDIG's substantive consolidation claim. All claims were resolved by a settlement agreement dated 12 August 2025 under which IREN paid $20 million. The Canadian receivership concluded September 2025, the Australian recognition proceedings terminated October 2025, and the Canadian bankruptcy proceedings concluded on 24 December 2025.1

Securities class action — dismissed, on appeal

A putative class action filed 14 December 2022 in the District of New Jersey alleged misstatements in the IPO registration statement and in the period to 1 November 2022. After two rounds of amendment, the court granted IREN's motion to dismiss in full and with prejudice on 18 February 2026. The lead plaintiffs noticed an appeal to the Third Circuit on 13 March 2026; briefing has not yet been scheduled.1

Canada Revenue Agency — open

IREN filed a Notice of Appeal in the Tax Court of Canada on 23 June 2025 disputing the CRA's determination that the company has a permanent establishment in Canada, and the related GST assessment. The Crown replied in November 2025. No hearing timetable is set.1

Audit trail

Raymond Chabot Grant Thornton served from May 2023 until dismissal on 26 November 2025, replaced by KPMG LLP the following day. There were no disagreements — but RCGT's FY2024 report carried a going-concern explanatory paragraph, and RCGT reported a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting disclosed in the FY2024 Form 20-F. Both are historical; the FY2026 10-K reports management's assessment with auditor attestation under Section 404(b).11114
