**Fallback Plan: If Commissioners Vote NO**

**Voter Education and Ballot Campaign Plan for the Emerald ProjectCo Data Center Tax Abatement Proposal**

| County | Pittsburg County, Oklahoma |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Commission Vote Date** | June 22, 2026 |
| **Project Name** | Emerald ProjectCo Data Center Economic Development Project Plan |
| **Tax Districts** | Proposed Tax Abatement District No. 1 and Tax Abatement District No. 2 |
| **Fallback Trigger** | Commissioners vote NO, then IREN / Emerald ProjectCo or related proponents attempt to use the initiative petition process or another ballot route to approve the abatement plan. |

**Campaign North Star: This is not anti-growth. This is pro-fair-deal, pro-transparency, and pro-local-control.**

**1\. Executive Summary**

If the Board of County Commissioners votes NO on June 22, 2026, the immediate objective is to defend that outcome and prepare for the possibility that IREN / Emerald ProjectCo or its supporters attempt to petition the tax abatement proposal onto the ballot. The campaign must shift from collecting referendum signatures to educating voters, organizing supporters, and preparing to win a countywide vote against the proposed tax abatement.

The public message should stay consistent: Pittsburg County can support responsible economic development while rejecting an 85% tax abatement plan that has not demonstrated enough enforceable public benefit, transparency, and accountability.

**2\. Strategic Goal**

Secure a countywide NO vote if the proposed Emerald ProjectCo Data Center Economic Development Project Plan, Tax Abatement District No. 1, Tax Abatement District No. 2, or related tax abatement measure is placed on the ballot after a Commissioner NO vote.

**Primary Objectives**

* Define the issue before the project proponents do.  
* Keep the campaign focused on transparency, fairness, accountability, and local control.  
* Make the ballot choice easy to understand: a NO vote rejects the special tax abatement plan.  
* Educate voters without sounding anti-business or anti-growth.  
* Build a visible, countywide coalition of taxpayers, parents, small businesses, retirees, public service supporters, and local control advocates.  
* Track voter contacts and convert undecided voters before early voting and election day.

**3\. Trigger Points and Decision Tree**

| Scenario | Immediate Response | Next Action |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Commissioners vote YES | Activate referendum signature plan. | File true copy, print filed pamphlets, train circulators, collect signatures. |
| Commissioners vote NO and proponents do nothing | Thank Commissioners and continue monitoring. | Keep the voter education network warm; prepare for future agenda items. |
| Commissioners vote NO and proponents announce a petition | Switch to ballot campaign mode immediately. | Launch a public education plan, track their petition, prepare challenge review, and recruit voter contact volunteers. |
| Proponents file petition language or ballot title | Obtain the filing and review exact wording. | Analyze ballot title, measure text, deadlines, and legal sufficiency with counsel. |
| Measure qualifies for ballot | Begin full Vote NO campaign. | Launch canvassing, mail/social media plan, public forums, earned media, and turnout program. |

**4\. Message Frame**

Do not let the project proponents frame the campaign as jobs versus anti-growth residents. The campaign frame should be:

**Responsible growth should pay its fair share. Pittsburg County voters deserve a fair deal, full transparency, and enforceable public benefit before approving a major tax abatement.**

**Core Message**

We support smart growth, but we oppose giving a major tax abatement to a private data center project without clear, enforceable public benefit. Pittsburg County voters deserve transparency, accountability, and a fair deal.

**Three Message Pillars**

| Pillar | Meaning | Plain-Language Message |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Transparency | The public deserves the full deal and all agreements before being asked to approve tax relief. | Show us the deal. Show us the numbers. Show us the guarantees. |
| Fairness | Residents and local small businesses do not receive an 85% tax abatement. | Why should regular taxpayers pay full freight while a private data center receives a special deal? |
| Accountability | Any tax relief must include enforceable promises, clawbacks, public reporting, and real local benefit. | If they want public tax relief, they need enforceable public benefit. |

**Recommended Slogans**

* Let the People Vote. Then Vote NO.  
* No Blank Check for Big Data.  
* Fair Growth, Not Special Deals.  
* Growth Should Pay Its Way.  
* Pittsburg County Deserves the Full Deal, Not the Sales Pitch.  
* Vote NO on the Proposed Data Center Tax Abatement Plan.

**5\. First 72 Hours After a Commissioner NO Vote**

1. Issue a public statement thanking the Commissioners for listening and warning voters that the company may try to override the NO vote through a petition or ballot effort.  
2. Launch the fallback campaign identity: name, logo, Facebook group/page, Signal group, email list, and volunteer sign-up form.  
3. Create a simple one-page explainer titled: "What Happened and What Comes Next?"  
4. Assign a research lead to collect every public document: project plan, draft resolution, review committee recommendations, tax district maps, agreements, exhibits, meeting minutes, and statements by IREN / Emerald ProjectCo.  
5. Assign a petition-watch lead to monitor filings with the County Election Board, County Clerk, and Board of County Commissioners.  
6. Assign a communications lead to maintain message discipline and approve public posts.  
7. Begin capturing voter data: name, precinct or town, contact method, support level, and volunteer interest.  
8. Prepare a public records request list if documents are missing or vague.

**6\. Public Statement Template**

Use this language immediately after a Commissioner NO vote if proponents indicate they may petition the measure onto the ballot:

| The Commissioners made the right call by refusing to approve a major tax abatement plan without enough public proof and enforceable guarantees. If IREN / Emerald ProjectCo or its supporters attempt to force this issue onto the ballot, we will organize a countywide voter education campaign. This is not about opposing growth. It is about protecting Pittsburg County taxpayers, local services, schools, roads, emergency services, and local control. If the deal is good for the county, they should be willing to show the full deal, answer public questions, and prove the public benefit. |
| :---- |

**7\. Research Questions the Campaign Must Answer**

* What exact tax relief or abatement is being requested?  
* How much tax revenue is deferred, captured, exempted, or reduced?  
* Which taxing jurisdictions are affected, including schools, county government, roads, emergency services, and other local entities?  
* How many permanent local jobs are guaranteed in writing?  
* What wages, benefits, and hiring commitments are guaranteed?  
* What water, electric, road, emergency service, and infrastructure demands are expected?  
* What clawbacks exist if the company does not deliver promised jobs or investment?  
* What happens if the company sells, restructures, transfers the project, or changes the business model?  
* What public reporting is required during the abatement period?  
* Who benefits financially from the districts and agreements?  
* What is the total public cost over the life of the abatement?  
* What alternatives were considered, such as a smaller abatement, shorter term, or performance-based tax relief?

**8\. Likely Proponent Arguments and Responses**

| Their Argument | Disciplined Response |
| :---- | :---- |
| This brings jobs. | How many permanent local jobs are guaranteed in writing, at what wage, and with what clawbacks if they do not deliver? |
| This brings investment. | Investment is not the same as public benefit. If the public gives up tax revenue, voters deserve to know the return. |
| This expands the tax base long term. | Then show the numbers, the timeline, and what local services lose or defer during the abatement period. |
| Opponents are anti-growth. | We are not anti-growth. We are pro-fair-deal. Growth should pay its way. |
| Other counties give incentives. | Pittsburg County voters get to decide what is right for Pittsburg County. |
| The project will not happen without the abatement. | Then voters deserve to see the full financial case before giving a special tax deal. |

**10\. Voter Contact Program**

The campaign should not rely on Facebook alone. The win condition is direct contact with Pittsburg County voters.

**Voter Contact Categories**

| Category | Meaning | Follow-Up |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Strong NO | Committed to voting against the abatement plan. | Recruit as volunteer, yard sign host, or messenger. |
| Lean NO | Likely opposed but needs reinforcement. | Send FAQ, invite to forum, follow up before voting. |
| Undecided | Needs information and trust-building. | Ask what they care about, provide tailored facts, follow up. |
| Lean YES | Currently supportive but persuadable. | Ask about transparency, guarantees, and public cost. |
| Strong YES | Supportive of the abatement. | Do not waste time arguing unless influential. |

**Contact Methods**

* Door knocking in priority neighborhoods and towns.  
* Phone calls to known supporters and undecided voters.  
* Text outreach only if legally and operationally appropriate.  
* Tabling at public events, community spaces, and local gatherings.  
* Letters to the editor and local radio call-ins if available.  
* Small-group presentations for churches, civic clubs, parent groups, retirees, and small business owners.

**12\. Public Forum Strategy**

Publicly invite IREN / Emerald ProjectCo and county officials to answer voter questions. If they decline, the campaign should still hold voter education forums and document unanswered questions.

**Forum Format**

* Open with a neutral explanation of the ballot issue.  
* Present the known facts from public documents.  
* List unanswered questions clearly.  
* Invite questions from residents.  
* Collect voter contact information and volunteer sign-ups.  
* Avoid turning the forum into a shouting match.

**Questions to Ask Proponents**

* What is the total estimated public tax value of the abatement over its full term?  
* How many permanent local jobs are guaranteed in writing?  
* What clawbacks apply if job or investment targets are not met?  
* Which local taxing entities lose, defer, or redirect revenue?  
* What infrastructure costs will be borne by the company versus taxpayers?  
* Will all agreements be released before voting begins?  
* What happens if the project is sold, assigned, or transferred?

**What Not to Say**

* Do not say the project will definitely destroy school funding unless you have a sourced number.  
* Do not say the data center itself is illegal or bad unless the campaign has adopted that claim.  
* Do not promise that a NO vote stops every possible version of the project.  
* Do not attack workers, local supporters, or people who want economic development.  
* Do not speculate about motives. Stick to documents, numbers, and public accountability.

**15\. Compliance and Legal Watch Notes**

This section is not legal advice. It is an operational checklist for issues that should be reviewed by counsel or the proper election officials as the situation develops.

* Confirm whether proponents use an initiative petition, referendum petition, or another ballot route.  
* Obtain the exact petition text, ballot title, gist, and proponents of record.  
* Track all filing dates, protest windows, signature deadlines, and election dates.  
* Review whether campaign finance reporting or disclaimer requirements apply to the voter education effort.  
* Do not interfere with lawful petition circulation by other groups. Educate voters and challenge legally through proper channels if necessary.  
* Document misleading statements with screenshots, dates, sources, and witnesses.

**16\. Source Notes for Petition Context**

These notes are included to keep the campaign team oriented. Update them if election officials, counsel, or the final filing uses a different route.

* 62 O.S. § 868 reserves initiative and referendum powers to city, town, and county voters for Local Development Act tax relief, incentives, exemptions, or increment capture.  
* 34 O.S. § 2 provides an Oklahoma initiative petition form and states that county, city, or municipal initiative petition timing is ninety days.  
* 34 O.S. § 1 provides the referendum petition form and states that county, city, or municipal referendum timing is thirty days.  
* 34 O.S. § 3 describes petition pamphlets and requires signature sheets to be attached to a copy of the petition.  
* 34 O.S. § 6 covers circulator verification by affidavit.
