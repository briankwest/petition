# **The Goal**

If the County Commissioners approve the 85% tax abatement, the goal is to refer that resolution to the registered voters of the county for approval or rejection. The process must be exact, documented, and boring. Technical mistakes can give the county, developer, or project supporters a path to challenge the petition.

# **1\. Vote Night: June 22**

\[ \] Stay until the vote is complete and the final motion is clear.

\[ \] Write down the resolution number, exact title, and whether it was amended before passage.

\[ \] Record who voted yes, who voted no, and whether anyone abstained or was absent.

\[ \] Ask for the final adopted resolution and all attachments before leaving, if available.

\[ \] Do not collect signatures at the meeting unless the true copy has already been filed and the official petition pamphlets are ready.

# **2\. Get the Adopted Resolution and Attachments**

\[ \] Request the file-stamped or certified adopted resolution from the County Clerk or Board of County Commissioners.

\[ \] Request any project plan, development agreement, abatement schedule, Local Development Act documents, maps, exhibits, and tax-impact documents.

\[ \] Use the final adopted version, not a draft agenda packet version.

\[ \] Scan and save all documents immediately. Keep originals and certified copies secure.

\[ \] If the title, number, or text differs from the draft, update the petition to match the adopted version exactly.

# **3\. Get the Registered Voter Count**

\[ \] Ask the County Election Board for the current total number of registered voters residing in the county.

\[ \] Calculate 10% of that number as the minimum legal target under 62 O.S. § 868\.

\[ \] Set the real collection goal higher, ideally 13% to 15% of registered voters, because some signatures will be invalidated.

\[ \] Document who gave the voter count, when, and in what form.

\[ \] Save an email or written response if possible.

# **4\. Finalize the Petition Packet**

\[ \] Insert the county name everywhere it appears.

\[ \] Insert the resolution number, title, and exact adoption date.

\[ \] Attach or insert the exact text of the adopted resolution and all required attachments.

\[ \] Confirm the gist is accurate, neutral, and not argumentative.

\[ \] Confirm the proposed ballot title is neutral, understandable, and does not argue for or against the measure.

\[ \] Name up to three proponents of record.

# **5\. File Before Circulating**

\[ \] Bring at least two complete copies of the finalized referendum measure to the Secretary of the County Election Board.

\[ \] Ask the office to file-stamp both copies with the date and time.

\[ \] Leave one copy with the office and keep one file-stamped copy for your records.

\[ \] Record the date, time, office location, and person who accepted the filing.

\[ \] Only after filing should the group print and circulate identical petition pamphlets.

# **6\. Print Identical Petition Pamphlets**

\[ \] Do not let volunteers create, edit, or print their own versions.

\[ \] Each signature sheet must remain attached to a copy of the petition packet.

\[ \] Number each pamphlet and log who receives it.

\[ \] Do not circulate loose signature pages.

\[ \] Do not detach pages for convenience.

\[ \] Print extra complete pamphlets rather than adding loose sheets later.

# **7\. Train Circulators Before Collection Starts**

\[ \] Each circulator should be a registered Oklahoma voter.

\[ \] The circulator must personally witness each signature on the sheet they circulate.

\[ \] Signers must use their own legal name and voter-registration address information.

\[ \] No one may sign for a spouse, parent, neighbor, or friend.

\[ \] The circulator affidavit should be completed only after signatures on that sheet are collected.

\[ \] Every affidavit must be notarized correctly.

# **8\. Collect Signatures Carefully**

\[ \] Each signer should be a registered voter residing in the county.

\[ \] Each signer should enter legal first name, legal last name, house number, street, city or town, ZIP code, and birth month/day in MM/DD format.

\[ \] Review each line before the signer walks away, but do not alter the signer’s information yourself.

\[ \] If something is wrong, have the signer correct it personally.

\[ \] Keep original pamphlets clean, flat, and secure.

\[ \] Return completed pamphlets to the petition captain daily or every few days.

# **9\. Notarize, Audit, and Overcollect**

\[ \] Check every affidavit for circulator name, signature, county, date, notary signature, notary seal, commission number, and commission expiration.

\[ \] Flag incomplete signatures, duplicates, illegible entries, and questionable voter-registration matches.

\[ \] Count only signatures that appear complete and likely valid when deciding whether you have enough.

\[ \] Overcollect. The minimum legal number is not the safe number.

\[ \] Keep a master log of pamphlet numbers, circulators, signature counts, and return dates.

# **10\. File Signed Petitions Before the Deadline**

\[ \] If the resolution passes on June 22, 2026, plan as if the deadline is July 22, 2026\.

\[ \] Do not wait until the final hour of the final day.

\[ \] Bring all original signed petition pamphlets, organized and logged.

\[ \] Ask for a receipt or file-stamped proof of submission.

\[ \] Keep copies or scans of your logs and any receipt. Preserve the originals exactly as submitted.

# **11\. Prepare for a Challenge**

\[ \] Assume someone may challenge the wording, ballot title, signature validity, circulator affidavits, attached pages, or timing.

\[ \] Keep a written record of every filing, office visit, public-records request, print batch, packet handoff, and notarization.

\[ \] Keep public messaging accurate and avoid overstating what the petition does.

\[ \] Use one official source for instructions to volunteers.

\[ \] If possible, have an Oklahoma election-law attorney review the petition before circulation.

**\[NO\] Do not collect signatures before the true copy is filed with the Secretary of the County Election Board.**

**\[NO\] Do not use homemade sheets, Facebook lists, notebook paper, Google Forms, or informal signup sheets as legal petition signatures.**

**\[NO\] Do not circulate loose signature sheets.**

**\[NO\] Do not detach signature sheets from the pamphlet.**

**\[NO\] Do not let volunteers change the petition wording.**

**\[NO\] Do not let someone sign for another person.**

# **Source Notes**

**62 O.S. § 868:** Local Development Act initiative and referendum power for tax relief, incentives, exemptions, and captured increments. It includes county filing with the secretary of the county election board, the 10% registered-voter threshold, and 30-day referendum timing after adoption. https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-62/section-62-868/

**34 O.S. § 1:** Referendum petition form and county/local 30-day timing language. https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-34/section-34-1/

**34 O.S. § 3:** Petition pamphlet and signature-sheet attachment requirements, warning language, gist, and public-records notice. https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-34/section-34-3/

**34 O.S. § 6:** Circulator verification and affidavit requirement. https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-34/section-34-6/

**34 O.S. § 6.1:** Common signature-sheet and notarization defects that can lead to exclusion. https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-34/section-34-6-1/

# **Legal Caution**

This is an organizing checklist and document-preparation aid, not legal advice. Oklahoma petition law is technical. Before circulation, verify the final packet, filing location, deadline, and ballot title with the County Election Board and, if possible, an Oklahoma election-law attorney.
