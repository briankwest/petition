# Referendum Toolkit — Implementation Plan

**Pittsburg County, Oklahoma · Referendum on the Emerald ProjectCo Data Center Tax Abatement (85%)**
Drafted 2026-09-01. Statute text quoted below was pulled this day from OSCN (Title 34) and the Oklahoma Legislature's official Title 62 PDF; full verbatim extracts are in `reference/statutes/`. This is an engineering and organizing plan, not legal advice — every legal instrument produced here goes to an Oklahoma election-law attorney and the County Election Board Secretary before it is filed or printed.

> **Revisions, 2026-09-01 (afternoon), after the go-ahead:**
> 1. **The Board tabled the resolution.** No adoption date, filing deadline, election date, or registered-voter count exists. All are `null` placeholders in `config/petition.yaml`; every generator renders them as bracketed `[… — TBD]` text and `make final` refuses to build until they are real. "June 22 / July 22" no longer appear anywhere.
> 2. **The web app is the Petition Master system of record.** The captain keeps pamphlets, sheets, circulators, issues, events and settings current in `/admin` (Postgres on Dokku); the public site shows live signature counts (opt-in flags). The XLSX is an **export** of that database (same sheet layout and formulas as the captain's existing `Petition Captain Master Tracker.xlsx`, which is imported once). Signer PII is never stored — tracking stays at pamphlet/sheet level.
> 3. **Hosting:** Dokku + Postgres, `petition.mcalester.net`. Any request arriving at another host (misspelled domains are attached in Dokku) is 301-redirected to the canonical host. See `DEPLOY.md`.
> 4. Sections 7, 9 and 12 below should be read with those changes; the statutory sections are unchanged.

## 1. Legal framework (quoted) and what each rule means for the build

### 1.1 The governing statute: 62 O.S. § 868 (Local Development Act)

This is the section that gives county voters the referendum right over Local Development Act tax abatements and sets the county-specific procedure. Quoted from `reference/statutes/62-868.txt`:

> **A.** The powers of initiative and referendum, reserved by the Oklahoma Constitution to the people, are reserved to the people of every city, town or county with reference to the tax relief or incentives or exemptions or increment captured as authorized by Section 6C of Article X of the Oklahoma Constitution and as provided for in this act.
>
> **B. 1.** For purposes of this section, the form of the petition for either initiative or referendum shall be substantially as provided in Sections 1 and 2 of Title 34 of the Oklahoma Statutes. A true copy of each measure proposed by initiative and referendum shall be filed with the clerk of the city or town or with the secretary of the county election board before it is circulated and signed by the registered voters.
>
> **2.** Every petition for either the initiative or referendum shall be signed by a number of the registered voters residing in the city or town equal to at least twenty-five percent (25%) of the total number of votes cast at the preceding general municipal election or for counties, equal to at least ten percent (10%) of the registered voters residing in the county. The signatures to each petition shall be verified in the manner provided by law.
>
> **3.** … Signed copies of a petition invoking a referendum upon any ordinance or resolution shall be submitted to the clerk or secretary within thirty (30) days after the passage or adoption of the ordinance or resolution.
>
> **C.** When signed copies of a petition are timely filed with the clerk or secretary, the clerk or secretary shall make a physical count of the number of signatures appearing on the petitions. He shall then publish, in at least one newspaper of general circulation in the municipality or the county, a notice of the filing and the apparent sufficiency or insufficiency of the petition. … A protest to the petition or the count of signatures shall be filed in the district court in the county … within ten (10) days after the publication. …
>
> **D. 1.** The parties submitting a petition for either initiative or referendum shall also prepare and file a ballot title for the measure. The ballot title may be filed with the clerk or secretary prior to circulating the petition, but it must be submitted no later than the time that the signed copies of the petition are filed with the clerk or secretary. The ballot title shall contain the gist of the proposition couched in language that may be readily understood by persons not engaged in the practice of law. The ballot title shall contain language which clearly states that a "yes" vote is a vote in favor of the proposition, and a "no" vote is a vote against the proposition. The ballot title may not: a. exceed one hundred fifty words, b. reflect partiality in its composition or contain any argument for or against the measure, or c. contain language whereby a "yes" vote is, in fact, a vote against the proposition and a "no" vote is, in fact, a vote in favor of the proposition.
>
> **2.** The clerk or secretary shall immediately forward a copy of the proposition and ballot title to the municipal attorney or district attorney. Within three (3) days after the filing of the ballot title, the attorney shall notify the clerk or secretary in writing whether or not the proposed ballot title is in legal form and in harmony with the law. If the ballot title is not in proper form, in the opinion of the attorney, he shall prepare and file a ballot title which does conform to the law within the three-day period.
>
> **E.** A qualified elector who is dissatisfied with the wording of a ballot title may appeal, within ten (10) days after the ballot title is filed with the clerk or secretary, to the district court …
>
> **H.** Whenever a referendum is demanded against any measure passed by the city, town or county governing body, … the question shall be submitted to the registered voters of the city, town or county for their approval or rejection at the next general municipal or county election.

**What this drives in the build**

| Rule | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Form "substantially as provided in Sections 1 and 2 of Title 34" | The pamphlet template is built line-by-line from the § 1 form (§ 1.2 below). Deviations are documented, not improvised. |
| True copy filed with the **Secretary of the County Election Board** *before* circulation | Build has a `--final` mode that refuses to render while any placeholder remains; after filing, the PDF is frozen and hashed (§ 10). |
| Threshold = **10% of registered voters residing in the county** | The workbook computes the legal minimum from the Election Board's written count and a 15% working target. Note: the census population figures in our GIS data (43,773 total / 34,104 voting-age, 2020) are **not** registered-voter counts — do not use them for the threshold. |
| Signed copies due **within 30 days after adoption** | `deadline = adoption_date + 30 days` is computed, never typed. Workbook shows days remaining. |
| County publishes notice; **10-day** protest window in district court | Deadline tracker includes it; the records log captures the publication date. |
| Ballot title: **≤ 150 words**, gist, yes/no language, no partiality; may be filed before circulation; DA reviews in **3 days**; 10-day appeal | Ballot title is a separate document (D2), word-counted and linted by script. File it with the true copy so the DA review happens before circulation, not during the count. |
| Election at the **next general county election** | The petition form's election-date blank is filled from config after confirming with the Election Board/counsel (open question Q3). |

### 1.2 Petition form: 34 O.S. § 1 (as amended 2024)

> **A.** The referendum petition shall be substantially as follows:
>
> PETITION FOR REFERENDUM
>
> To the Honorable ____________, Governor of Oklahoma (or To the Honorable ____________, Mayor, Chairman of County Commissioners, or other chief executive officer, as the case may be, of the city, county or other municipal corporation of ____________):
>
> We, the undersigned legal voters of the State of Oklahoma (or district of __________, county of ____________, or city of ____________, as the case may be), respectfully order that Senate (or House) Bill No. __________ (or ordinance No. ____________), entitled (title of Act, and if the petition is against less than the whole Act, then set forth here the part or parts on which the referendum is sought), passed by the ____________ Legislature of the State of Oklahoma, at the regular (or special) session of said legislature, shall be referred to the people of the State (district of ____________, county of ____________, or city of ____________, as the case may be) for their approval or rejection at the regular (or special) election to be held on the ________ day of ____________, 20__, and each for himself says: I have personally signed this petition; I am a legal voter of the State of Oklahoma (and district of __________, county of ____________, or city of ____________, as the case may be); the following five data points shall be included on the form: the voter's legal first name, legal last name, zip code, house number and numerical month and day of my birth.
>
> Referendum petitions shall be filed with the Secretary of State not more than ninety (90) days after the final adjournment of the session of the legislature which passed the bill on which the referendum is demanded. (For county, city or other municipality the length of time shall be thirty (30) days.)
>
> The question we herewith submit to our fellow voters is: Shall the following bill of the legislature (or ordinance or resolution--local legislation) be approved? (Insert here an exact copy of the text of the measure followed by proponents of record (not to exceed three signatures, complete printed name and address associated with his or her Oklahoma voter registration record).)
>
> **B.** In order for the signature to be approved by the Secretary of State, four or more data points described in subsection A of this section must be matched to the voter registration file.

**Consequences**
- The pamphlet's petition text will track this form clause-for-clause, with the county variants selected ("Chairman of County Commissioners", "county of Pittsburg", "Resolution No. ___", "resolution--local legislation").
- The signer attestation — *"and each for himself says: I have personally signed this petition; I am a legal voter of the State of Oklahoma and of the county of Pittsburg"* — **is missing from the current draft** and will be added.
- The **five statutory data points** are: legal first name, legal last name, ZIP code, house number, numerical month and day of birth. Our signature columns carry all five plus signature, street name, and city (the affidavit swears to "mailing address"). "Birth MM/DD only" in the existing drafts is correct.
- The **4-of-5 match rule** (subsection B) is the Secretary of State's standard; the county secretary verifies "in the manner provided by law" and will almost certainly apply the same test. The Quick Card will tell circulators exactly which five fields decide validity.
- The 30-day county filing language and the computed expiration date go on the petition.

### 1.3 Pamphlet, warning, gist, public-records notice: 34 O.S. § 3 (as amended 2025)

> **A.** Each initiative petition and each referendum petition shall be duplicated for the securing of signatures. The Secretary of State shall design a form, subject to change, for signatures that shall be used by proponents of initiatives or referendums when collecting signatures, and each sheet for signatures shall be attached to a copy of the petition. Each copy of the petition and sheets for signatures is hereinafter termed a pamphlet. On the outer page of each pamphlet shall be printed the word "Warning", and underneath this in ten-point type the words, "It is a felony for anyone to sign an initiative or referendum petition with any name other than his or her own, or knowingly to sign his or her name more than once for the measure, or to sign such petition when he or she is not a legal voter of this state." A statement of the gist of the proposition shall be printed on the top margin of each signature sheet and shall:
> 1. Explain in basic words, which can be easily found in dictionaries of general usage, the effect of the proposition;
> 2. Not contain any words which have a special meaning for a particular profession or trade not commonly known to the citizens of this state;
> 3. Not contain euphemisms, words, or phrases regarded in popular parlance as code words, or an apparent attempt to deceive voters;
> 4. Not reflect partiality in its composition or contain any argument for or against the measure; and
> 5. Indicate whether a proposed measure will have a fiscal impact on the state and if so, the potential source of funding …
>
> **B.** A statement shall be printed under the gist of the proposition that provides notice that a copy of the petition and all signatures on such petition are public records subject to the Oklahoma Open Records Act.

**Consequences**
- The Warning is the **outer page** of every pamphlet, verbatim, at ≥ 10-point (we use 12-point under a 16-point "Warning"). The checker compares the rendered string to the statute text byte-for-byte.
- Gist at the **top margin of every signature sheet**, identical on every sheet, plus the Open Records notice directly under it. The checker extracts the gist from each signature page and fails if any two differ.
- Every signature sheet is physically part of a pamphlet (a bound copy of the full petition + measure). The build never emits a standalone signature page; the pamphlet is one PDF.
- Item 5 (state fiscal impact) is written for state questions; for a county measure the gist should still say plainly what the resolution does (an 85% ad valorem abatement for a defined project and districts) without argument. We will ask the Election Board Secretary for the **current Secretary of State signature-sheet form** and mirror its column layout so the sheet looks like what verifiers already know.

### 1.4 Circulator affidavit: 34 O.S. § 6 (as amended 2025)

> Any person who circulates a sheet of the petition shall be a registered voter of this state and shall verify the signatures included on any signature sheets he or she circulates by executing his or her affidavit thereon and as a part thereof. The Secretary of State shall cause to be affixed onto the back of the signature form an affidavit, subject to change, for the circulator to verify the signatures.
>
> State of Oklahoma, ) ss. County of _______ )
>
> I, ______, being first duly sworn, say: That I am at least eighteen (18) years old, a registered voter of this state, and that all signatures on the signature sheet were signed in my presence; I believe that each has stated his or her name, mailing address, county of residence, and date of birth associated with his or her Oklahoma voter registration record, and that each signer is a legal voter of the State of Oklahoma and county of ______ or of the city of ______ (as the case may be). (Signature and complete address of affiant.)
>
> Subscribed and sworn to before me this ________ day of ________ A.D. 20__.
>
> (Signature and title of the Oklahoma notarial officer before whom oath is made, and his or her complete address, commission number and expiration date, and official Oklahoma notary public seal.)

**Consequences**
- Affidavit text is rendered **verbatim** (county filled in as "Pittsburg", city clause omitted) on the **back** of each signature sheet — hence duplex printing with a forced sheet/affidavit page pairing.
- Circulators **must be registered Oklahoma voters** — the workbook's Circulators sheet has a "registration verified (date/by)" column that must be filled before a pamphlet can be issued.
- Notary block must capture: signature, title, complete address, commission number, expiration date, seal. The template gives each its own labeled line and a 2 × 2 in clear area for the seal.

### 1.5 Why sheets get thrown out: 34 O.S. § 6.1

> **A.** The Secretary of State shall make or cause to be made a verification and count of the number of signatures on the petitions. In making such count, the Secretary of State shall not include in such total count:
> 1. All signatures on any sheet of any petition which is not verified by the person who circulated the sheet of the petition as provided in Section 6 of this title;
> 2. All signatures of nonresidents;
> 3. All signatures on a sheet that is not attached to a copy of the petition pamphlet;
> 4. All multiple signatures on any printed signature line;
> 5. All signatures not on a printed signature line;
> 6. Those signatures by a person who signs with any name other than his or her own or signs more than once;
> 7. All signatures on any sheet on which a notary has failed to sign, the seal of the notary is absent, the commission of the notary has expired or the expiration date is not on the signature sheet; and
> 8. Any signatures that cannot be verified by the Secretary of State with the Oklahoma State Election Board's public voter registration records.

**Consequences**
- These eight items become **defect codes E1–E8** in the workbook's audit sheets and the Notary Checklist's "do not accept" list.
- Items 1, 3 and 7 kill an **entire sheet**. That is why the default stays at **10 signatures per sheet** (configurable): a bad affidavit costs 10 signatures, not 20.
- Signature rows are tall, ruled, and numbered so items 4 and 5 are physically hard to violate.

### 1.6 Who may sign; penalties: 34 O.S. § 23 (as amended eff. Jan 1, 2026)

> Every person who is a qualified elector of the State of Oklahoma may sign a petition for the referendum or for the initiative for any measure upon which he is legally entitled to vote. Any person signing any name other than his own to any petition, or knowingly signing his name more than once for the same measure at one election, or who is not at the time of signing the same a legal voter of this state, or whoever falsely makes or willfully destroys a petition or any part thereof, … shall, upon conviction thereof, be guilty of a Class D3 felony offense …

**Consequence**: signer eligibility for this measure = registered voter **residing in Pittsburg County** (§ 868 B.2 + § 23). The Quick Card and app say exactly that, and point people to the OK Voter Portal to check *before* they come.

### 1.7 Safety net, not a strategy: 34 O.S. § 24

> The procedure herein prescribed is not mandatory, but if substantially followed will be sufficient. If the end aimed at can be attained and procedure shall be sustained, clerical and mere technical errors shall be disregarded.

We build as if this section did not exist.

### 1.8 State-only sections we mirror where harmless

- **34 O.S. § 9(B)** — for state measures the suggested ballot title "shall be filed on a separate sheet of paper and shall not be part of or printed on the petition." § 868(D) does not say this for counties, but nothing is gained by printing the ballot title inside the pamphlet and something could be lost. **Decision: ballot title is a separate document (D2), not printed in the pamphlet.** The current draft prints it inside — remove. (Confirm with counsel; Q4.)
- **34 O.S. § 8(E)** (state process) requires paid circulators to display a compensation disclosure. If any circulator is paid, we add the same disclosure line to the affidavit page. Default assumption: all volunteers (Q5).
- **34 O.S. § 2** is the initiative form (90-day window). Only relevant to the Fallback Plan if proponents try to initiate the abatement by petition after a NO vote.

## 5. Document generation (D1–D3)

### 5.1 Page geometry — all documents
- `@page { size: 8.5in 14in; margin: 0.75in }` (legal portrait, 612 × 1008 pt). Signature sheets and affidavits: `size: 14in 8.5in` (legal landscape).
- Body 12 pt / 1.4 line-height; headings 16–20 pt; nothing below 10 pt anywhere (statutory floor for the Warning; our floor for everything).
- Fonts embedded (Liberation Serif/Sans or similar metric-safe fonts) so the PDF is identical on every machine.
- Footer on every page: `Pittsburg County Referendum · Pamphlet No. ______ · Page X of Y`. No build hashes or dates in the footer of the pamphlet — anything printed becomes part of the filed instrument.

### 5.2 Pamphlet page order (`01-petition-pamphlet.pdf`)
1. **Cover / outer page** — "Warning" + statutory felony sentence, title block (county, measure short description, Resolution No., adoption date), "Use only the filed version — do not detach sheets", pamphlet number box.
2. **Petition for Referendum** — § 1 form, county variant, including signer attestation, five-data-points sentence, 30-day statement with the computed expiration date, and the question ("Shall the following resolution (local legislation) be approved?").
3. **Exact copy of the measure** — rendered from `measure/adopted-resolution.md` verbatim (title, recitals, sections, exhibits, project plan, district descriptions, any agreement adopted by reference). Padded with an intentionally blank page if needed so the first signature sheet starts on a front.
4. **Proponents of record** (≤ 3) — signature, printed name, registration address.
5. **Signature sheets × N** (default 5) — landscape; top margin: gist (bold, 12 pt) + Open Records notice + one-line signer instruction; table of 10 numbered rows × columns `# | Signature | Legal First Name | Legal Last Name | House # | Street Name | City/Town | ZIP | Birth MM/DD`; row height 0.5 in; the five statutory columns shaded/labeled "required for verification"; sheet header `Sheet N of 5 · Pamphlet No. ____`.
6. **Affidavit × N** — on the back of each sheet: header "Circulator Affidavit — verifies Sheet N of this pamphlet only", statutory affidavit verbatim (county = Pittsburg), affiant signature + printed name + complete address lines, notary block with labeled lines for signature, printed name/title, complete address, commission number, expiration date, and a 2 × 2 in seal area.

Column widths (landscape, 12.5 in usable): `#` 0.4 · Signature 2.6 · First 1.6 · Last 1.7 · House # 0.9 · Street 2.2 · City 1.3 · ZIP 0.9 · MM/DD 0.9.

### 5.3 Duplex control
Landscape duplex orientation depends on the printer driver ("flip on long edge" vs "short edge"). `build_docs.py --duplex short-edge` rotates every affidavit page 180° so the back reads upright for that setting. Phase 1 ends with a **physical proof print** of one pamphlet on the actual printer/vendor, checked with a ruler (page size) and by flipping (back orientation). The chosen setting is recorded in config and in the print order.

### 5.4 Other documents
- `02-ballot-title.pdf` — one legal page: ballot title, word count printed, proponents, filing block. Linted (§ 6).
- `03-circulator-quick-card.pdf` — legal, duplex, two large panels per side, 13–14 pt, adds "The five fields that decide validity" and defect codes.
- `04-notary-checklist.pdf` — legal, checklist + session log with rows tall enough to write in.
- `05-action-plan.pdf`, `06-fallback-plan.pdf` — legal, generated from the cleaned markdown (moved to `reference/source-docs/`, converted to Jinja/markdown templates) with dates, deadline, contacts, and Source Notes injected from config.

## 7. Petition Master workbook (D4) — `scripts/build_xlsx.py`

One `.xlsx`, generated, with real formulas and data validation so the Petition Captain works in Excel/LibreOffice/Google Sheets without touching Python. `--import prior.xlsx` copies existing rows forward when the template is regenerated.

| Sheet | Columns / logic |
| --- | --- |
| **Dashboard** | Registered voters (value, source, date) · adoption date · true-copy filed (date/time/receiver) · deadline `=adoption+30` · days left `=deadline-TODAY()` · legal minimum `=ROUNDUP(reg*10%,0)` · target `=ROUNDUP(reg*15%,0)` · pamphlets issued/returned/notarized/audited (COUNTIFS) · raw signatures · audited valid · validity rate · projected valid · gap to target · signatures/day needed · progress bars (conditional formatting) |
| **Deadlines** | Computed timeline: adoption → true copy + ballot title filed → circulation start → submission deadline → county count/publication (entered) → protest window (+10) → DA ballot-title review (+3) → appeal window (+10) → election. Each with statute cite. |
| **Pamphlet Log** | Pamphlet # (pre-numbered) · print batch · version hash (must equal the filed hash) · issued to (dropdown from Circulators) · issued date · returned date · sheets used · raw signatures (SUMIF from Sheet Log) · notarized? · audited? · status (Issued/Returned/Notarized/Audited/Filed) · notes |
| **Sheet Log** | Pamphlet # · sheet # · circulator · signature count · notary name · commission # · expiration · notarized date · defect codes E1–E8 (multi) · valid count · reviewer |
| **Circulators** | Name · phone · email · OK voter registration verified (date, by) · trained (date) · compensated? (disclosure flag) · pamphlets outstanding (COUNTIFS) · total signatures · notes. A pamphlet cannot be issued to a circulator whose registration is unverified (validation rule). |
| **Notary Log** | Same rows as the Notary Checklist session log (date, pamphlet #, sheet #, circulator, sig count, notary initials, notes). |
| **Signature Audit** | Pamphlet · sheet · line · legal first · legal last · house # · ZIP · MM/DD · data points matched (0–5) · defect codes · valid `=AND(matched>=4, defects="")` · duplicate flag (COUNTIFS on name+DOB) · reviewer · date. **Captain-only file; never in git, never on the website.** |
| **Filing & Records Log** | The Action Plan's records table as rows: item, office, person, date/time, documents, receipt/file-stamp obtained. |
| **Contacts** | Election Board, County Clerk, District Attorney, attorney, print vendor, notaries, media. |
| **Precincts** | 38 rows from the GeoJSON: precinct, polling place, 2020 population, voting-age population, commissioner district, signing locations count, signatures attributed (optional, from audit by ZIP/city). |

Print setup on every sheet: legal landscape, repeat header rows, fit to width. Formulas are locked; data cells are open. A `template` copy with no rows lives in `output/xlsx/`; the live copy lives only with the Petition Captain (`*.xlsx` is gitignored).

## 10. Post-filing freeze and print control

1. `make final` — builds with `--final`, runs all checks, produces `output/final/01-petition-pamphlet.pdf`.
2. Attorney + Election Board Secretary review the PDF. Fixes go through config/templates, never the PDF.
3. File the true copy (and the ballot title) with the Secretary of the County Election Board; obtain file-stamped copies; record date/time/person in the workbook.
4. `make freeze` — copies the filed PDF to `output/filed/`, writes `SHA256SUMS`, tags the repo `filed-YYYY-MM-DD`, and switches the checker into "match filed hash" mode: any subsequent build that differs from the filed pamphlet fails.
5. Every print order references the hash; the Pamphlet Log records the batch and hash; a sample from each batch is checked against the file-stamped copy page-by-page.
6. Pamphlets are pre-numbered by the print vendor or by a separate `--stamp` pass that only fills the "Pamphlet No." box (confirm with counsel that stamping a number in a printed blank does not alter the filed instrument — it is the same practice used for state petitions).
